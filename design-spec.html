david.world — design 01 · the desk portal

“This is my desk. Everything starts here.”

One cinematic hub, two worlds behind it, and a plain-HTML twin for anyone in a hurry. Below: every screen, interaction, and build stage — approve it and I'll write the formal spec.

01 · THE HUB

The Desk at 23:47

You arrive mid-push-in: a lamp-lit desk, rain on the window, a monitor glowing with stars. Seven things invite a click. Mouse moves the camera a few gentle degrees; every hotspot glides you closer.

1Monitor → the Galaxy. The screen already shows living stars; click and the camera dives through the glass.
2Handheld → the Village. Pick it up, it boots with an 8-bit jingle, and the pixel world takes over the screen.
3Paper tray → CV & contact. View or download the PDF CV; a letter in the tray opens contact (email copy + links).
4Photo frame → about me. A warm card: who you are, where you're from, what you care about — including one important date, July 16, sitting quietly in the personal details. (It matters. See the handheld.)
5Coffee mug → heart steam. Click the mug and the steam curls into a ♥ — for everyone who came to visit.
6Sticky notes → quick links. GitHub, Google Scholar, LinkedIn, email — for visitors who want the shortcuts.
7Music box → work playlist. Wind the little crank: it opens your favorite work-music list (with links) — and doubles as the sound toggle for the rain + lo-fi ambience.
intro 4s camera push-in · skippable ambience wound up via the music box · off by default idle subtle drift, steam, rain, screen flicker
02 · THROUGH THE MONITOR

A Young Universe, Still Forming

Your career, honestly told — works only, no tool lists: one star already burning, one comet touring the conferences, one protostar about to ignite, two nebulae where new work is condensing. Every future paper ignites a new star. And drifting at the edge, one dark cloud nobody is told about.

Navigation: drag to orbit, scroll to glide; tap a body to approach it. Everything is reachable in two moves — it's a small sky on purpose.
Reading a body: approaching opens its card in-place — title, venue, abstract, your role, link/DOI (paper), PDF (poster), status + collaborators (projects).
The growth ritual: content lives in one works.json; when your ongoing paper publishes, its protostar ignites into a star — a one-line edit.
The Secret Nebula: an unlabeled, barely-visible dark cloud at the edge of the sky (bottom-right in the mock). Click it → your idea archive: topics and sparks, academic or not, each opening into a short card. It's never announced — only the curious find it.
epoch marker “universe est. 2026” content works.json + ideas.json exit pull back → through the glass → desk
03 · THROUGH THE HANDHELD

The Village: Your Story as a Playable RPG

The handheld boots — then asks for a passcode: ✱✱✱✱, “the day it all began.” The answer hides in plain sight in the photo frame's personal details (07·16). Only visitors who bothered to know you get in; three wrong tries earns a gentle nudge toward the frame. Inside: the personal world. You are tiny David, home at last.

School: your school life — stories, photos, memories, anything from those years; told through classroom objects and NPCs rather than a list. (You'll feed me the material; it can start small and grow.)
Home: hobbies, photos as wall art, fun facts told by objects — the personal side lives here, discovered not listed.
Lab: ALL your working projects — the not-yet-papers live here as machines mid-experiment. Plus the arcade corner: an unmarked cabinet holding a faithful classic 15-Puzzle (4×4, slide to order). Only when someone presses start does the screen reveal: “record to beat — 26.00s, set in middle school.” Beat it → the machine prints a claim ticket → email your time to collect a hand-written letter from you.
Library: the paper and the poster on shelves — plus a whole wing for your writing: essays, posts, notes, anything you write. This is the archive of everything written-by-David.
Mailbox: the leave-me-a-message spot — write a letter in-game (pixel stationery), press send, and it arrives in your inbox. Quest-linked so most visitors will try it.
Quests & achievements: crack the passcode · beat the record · read something in the library · leave a letter · find the hidden coffee machine. Finishing unlocks a shareable “I made it into David's world” card.

04 · THE SIDE DOOR

Classic Mode — nobody gets left outside

A quiet “☰ classic site” button always floats in the corner. It flips to a plain, elegant one-page version: bio, publications, projects, CV, contact. Same content, zero spectacle, loads instantly.

Auto-offered when it matters: no WebGL, prefers-reduced-motion, very slow connection, or screen reader → the site suggests (or defaults to) Classic Mode instead of failing.
It's also the skeleton: the classic page is real HTML baked into index.html, so Google, previews, and copy-paste all work — the 3D mounts on top of it. Canvas-only sites are invisible to search engines; yours won't be.
SEO name + papers indexable a11y keyboard & screen-reader complete speed classic loads < 1s
05 · UNDER THE HOOD

How it's built — and how it grows

Boring on purpose: vanilla JavaScript + three.js, a tiny hand-rolled pixel engine, one JSON file of content, GitHub Pages with auto-deploy. No frameworks to rot; updating the site = editing one file.

Stack: three.js (desk + galaxy), hand-rolled canvas 2D (village), Vite build, GitHub Actions → GitHub Pages. No backend, no database, no login.
Content = data: profile.json (bio, links, birthday-for-the-lock) · works.json (papers/posters/projects) · ideas.json (secret nebula topics) · playlist.json (music box) · writings/ (markdown, auto-shelved in the library). Add a paper → a star ignites.
Performance budget: first load < 2 MB; worlds lazy-load on entry; 60fps desktop / 30fps phone targets; rendering pauses when the tab is hidden.
Share-ready: OpenGraph card (the desk at night) so the link looks stunning in chats; title: “David Z. — a website you can walk into.”
Verification each stage: Playwright screenshots (desktop + phone), Lighthouse accessibility & performance pass, no-WebGL fallback test, JSON schema check, link/PDF check. Puzzle fairness: seeded scrambles + a claim code (time + moves + date) in the ticket so you can sanity-check record claims.
Easter eggs (v1 list): heart-shaped coffee steam, the music box melody, a hidden p=0.049 sticky note, the secret idea nebula, the village coffee machine, a shooting star every ~90s in the galaxy.
