david.world — concept board 01

A space, not a page

Six directions for a personal site people explore like a place — walk it, fly it, play it. None of them scroll. Pick the world that feels like you; we can steal the best moments from the rest.

01

The Probability Landscape

“A world terraformed from the distributions you work in.”

Visitors hike a 3D terrain whose mountains literally are probability curves — a Gaussian range at the center, Kaplan–Meier cliffs stepping down to the sea, a skewed χ² valley. Each landmark opens a chapter: your CV waits at the summit (at μ, naturally), papers are camps pitched along the survival cliffs, and fog banks you walk through are confidence intervals.

Signature moment — a visitor realizes the mountain they're climbing is a bell curve, and your CV sits exactly at the mean.

02

The Data Observatory

“Every paper a star. Your career, a navigable galaxy.”

Your work floats as a universe of glowing points: publications are stars, research areas form constellations, co-authorships are threads of light between them. Visitors pilot a camera through the dark and land on any star to read the work. The killer feature: one click re-projects the entire sky by year, topic, or journal — because the galaxy is a real dataset.

Signature moment — hitting “reproject” and watching the whole night sky rearrange itself into a live scatterplot.

03

Pixel Research Village

“Your career as a cozy retro RPG town.”

A tiny pixel-David walks through a hand-built village: the Lab holds current projects, the Library shelves your publications, NPCs (collaborators, mentors) offer dialogue, and the mailbox is your contact form. Visitors get a quest log — “read 3 papers, find the hidden coffee machine” — with achievements for completing your story.

Signature moment — a pixel-you hands visitors their first quest: Collect all publications.

04

The Institute

“A first-person walk through the research institute of you.”

Push open the doors of a stylized institute: a gallery corridor where framed papers light up as you pass, a lab where each bench runs one of your projects as a live demo, a seminar room screening your talks, and your office at the end — CV on the desk, coffee still warm. WASD to walk, click to inspect.

Signature moment — papers lighting up one by one as you walk the gallery corridor.

05

The Body Atlas

“Biostatistics, toured from inside the body itself.”

A translucent human figure floats in the dark; each glowing organ is a wing of your work — trials at the heart, ML at the brain, models in the lungs (mapped to your actual research areas). Visitors ride the bloodstream between stations, Magic-School-Bus style, while an EKG ticks along the bottom as the site's pulse.

Signature moment — riding a red blood cell from the heart to the brain to switch research areas.

06

The Infinite Desk

“One desk at midnight. Every object, a chapter.”

A single cinematic scene: your desk, lamp-lit, night outside. The camera glides as visitors click around — the monitor (running a live regression) opens projects, the book stack opens publications, the framed photo tells your story, the coffee steam hides an easter egg. The smallest world on this board, and the most polishable.

Signature moment — noticing the tiny scatterplot on the desk monitor is actually fitting itself in real time.

uniqueness ●●●●○ effort ●●○○○ phone ●●●●○ three.js or CSS 3D
Worlds can be combined. The Observatory could be the hub you fly between rooms from; the Desk could be the last room of the Institute; the Village could be the phone version of any of them. — And every direction ships with Classic Mode: one keystroke (or one link) flips to a plain, fast, screen-reader-friendly page, so a recruiter in a hurry still gets everything. Being the most unique site in the world shouldn't cost you a single visitor.
